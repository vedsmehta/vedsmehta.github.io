A take is an opinion about a company. A thesis is a falsifiable claim about how
the world is changing, from which opinions about many companies follow. This is
the first of those. It carries a kill condition and a date, so it can be checked
later against what actually happened.

## the change

Frontier safety work is moving from something labs do for themselves to
something they must demonstrate to third parties who have real access and the
right to publish what they find.

That shift matters more than it sounds. An internal safety practice produces
judgment. An externally verified one has to produce evidence: records, test
artifacts, audit trails, things that survive somebody else reading them. Once a
claim has to be shown rather than asserted, the machinery of showing it becomes
a thing you can sell.

## what it makes valuable

Audit capacity. Evaluation supply that survives contamination, since a test the
model has already seen proves nothing. Evidence collection. Attestation of
training facts. Anything that converts an internal practice into a checkable
record.

And it makes less valuable the safety tooling whose only customer is a lab's own
research organization. That work gets built in house, or the team building it
gets hired, which is the same outcome with extra steps.

## why now

On September 12, 2026, Dario Amodei published *We Must Pace the Frontier*. Read
as a safety argument it is familiar. Read as a market document it is a list of
things that do not exist yet and that a named set of buyers will shortly be
obliged to buy.

Anthropic committed unilaterally to embedded third party evaluators with
employee-like access, desks and badges and company laptops, and the right to
publish on risk levels, incidents and practices without editorial control. The
essay then asks for a law requiring the same of every US frontier company. Sam
Altman agreed publicly the same day.

A unilateral commitment is a policy. A commitment matched across the two largest
labs, with a legislative ask attached, is the beginning of a market.

## the kill condition

If by **March 2027** no US bill text names third party audit, and the embedded
evaluator commitments have not extended past METR to a second and third
organization, then this was two blog posts rather than a market structure, and
T1 is wrong.

I will write that up rather than quietly retiring it.

## confidence

Medium. The commitment is real, public, and matched. The mechanism that converts
it into spending is not yet built, and the gap between "we have committed to
this" and "there is a line item for it" is where most regulatory theses die.

The honest counter is that labs may satisfy every one of these obligations with
internal headcount and a nonprofit or two, and never buy anything at all.
